## The rulebook

These are the patterns to recognise. The next step ("The Investigation") walks through them in a real, story-driven lab. Run the **before** and **after** pairs side by side and compare *scan count* and *event count* in the Job Inspector.

---

### 1. Always specify an index

> **Why it matters:** `index=*` searches every accessible index — including `_internal`, `_audit`, and any test data you forgot was there.

**Before — searches every index:**
<SplunkSearch spl={`index=* | head 10`} />

**After — scoped to one index:**
<SplunkSearch spl={`index=s4c_www | head 10`} />

---

### 2. Use a specific time range

> **Why it matters:** *All Time* scans every bucket regardless of age. Most labs only need a few minutes of recent data.

**Before — All Time:**
<SplunkSearch spl={`index=s4c_www | stats count by status`} />

**After — last 7 days:**
<SplunkSearch earliest="-7d" latest="now" spl={`index=s4c_www | stats count by status`} />

---

### 3. Wildcards — leading and middle are slow

> **Why it matters:** Leading or middle wildcards bypass the bloom filter and force a full lexicon scan. Use `TERM()` for IP addresses and any value containing minor breakers.

**Before — middle wildcard, full lexicon scan:**
<SplunkSearch spl={`index=s4c_www *error*`} />

**After — TERM() uses the segmenter directly:**
<SplunkSearch spl={`index=s4c_www TERM(error)`} />

---

### 4. Filter before you transform

> **Why it matters:** Streaming commands (`eval`, `where`, `fields`) run on the indexers and reduce data *before* it reaches the search head. Transforming commands (`stats`, `chart`, `timechart`) need the full result set in one place.

**Before — transform first, filter later:**
<SplunkSearch spl={`index=s4c_www | stats count by status | where status>=400`} />

**After — filter at the source, then transform:**
<SplunkSearch spl={`index=s4c_www status>=400 | stats count by status`} />

---

### 5. `transaction` vs `stats`

> **Why it matters:** `transaction` without parameters runs entirely on the search head and is not distributed. Use `stats ... by <key>` unless you genuinely need `maxspan`, `startswith`, or event ordering.

**Before — search head only:**
<SplunkSearch spl={`index=s4c_www | transaction clientip`} />

**After — distributed, faster:**
<SplunkSearch spl={`index=s4c_www | stats values(uri) as uris, count by clientip`} />

---

### 6. `join` vs `stats` + `appendcols`

> **Why it matters:** `join` is sequential and the inner subsearch is capped at 50,000 rows by default. A `stats`-based pattern is parallel and avoids the cap entirely.

**Before — `join` with a subsearch:**
<SplunkSearch spl={`index=s4c_www | join clientip [search index=s4c_www status>=400 | table clientip status]`} />

**After — single pass with `stats`:**
<SplunkSearch spl={`index=s4c_www | stats count(eval(status>=400)) as errors, count as total by clientip`} />

---

### 7. Move `| search` before the first pipe

> **Why it matters:** `| search` after the first pipe runs on the search head, after every event has already arrived from the indexers. Same filter, much later.

**Before — filter at the search head:**
<SplunkSearch spl={`index=s4c_www | search status>=400`} />

**After — filter at the indexer:**
<SplunkSearch spl={`index=s4c_www status>=400`} />

---

## Patterns to avoid (no easy before/after)

> **`NOT` / `!=`** — Bloom filters and the segmenter find presence, not absence. Rewrite as a positive filter where possible: `(status=404 OR status=500)` instead of `status!=200`.

> **Real-time searches** — Continuous load on every search peer. Replace with a short-interval scheduled search and a panel that auto-refreshes.

> **Verbose search mode** — Sends full raw events to the search head even when you only need stats. Use *Smart* or *Fast* mode for any search that ends in an aggregation.

> **`savedsearch` re-runs from scratch** — Use `loadjob` to reuse a finished job's results instead of paying the search cost again.

> **Zero-result subsearches** — A subsearch returning no rows can yield false positives downstream. Guard the outer search with `require` (Splunk 9.0+) so it fails fast when the subsearch is empty.
