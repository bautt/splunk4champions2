## Search examples: Weather + Stock Correlation (no `join`)

This section focuses on **educational correlation patterns** between:
- `s4c_stock_indices` (market OHLCV events)
- `s4c_meteo_historic` (daily weather events)

Shared key:
- `exchange_city` (stocks) ↔ `city` (weather)
- `date` (or `_time` converted to `%Y-%m-%d`)

### Why avoid `join` here

For large historical ranges, `join` is usually slower and easier to truncate.  
In these examples, we use a scalable pattern:
1. Aggregate each dataset to the same key (`day`, `city`)
2. `append` weather aggregates
3. Merge with `stats values(...) by day city`
4. Continue analysis with `stats`, `streamstats`, or `tstats`

---

### Example 1 — Temperature regime vs daily stock return

Question: **Do returns look different on cold/warm/hot days per city?**

<SplunkSearch earliest="-10y" latest="now" spl={`index=s4c_stock_indices
| eval city=mvindex(exchange_city,0), day=coalesce(mvindex(date,0), strftime(_time, "%Y-%m-%d")), o=tonumber(mvindex(open,0)), c=tonumber(mvindex(close,0)), ret_pct=((c-o)/o)*100
| stats avg(ret_pct) as avg_return_pct by day city
| append [ search index=s4c_meteo_historic
    | eval day=coalesce(mvindex(date,0), strftime(_time, "%Y-%m-%d")), city=mvindex(city,0), temp_max_c=tonumber(temperature_2m_max), rain_mm=tonumber(precipitation_sum)
    | stats avg(temp_max_c) as temp_max_c avg(rain_mm) as rain_mm by day city ]
| stats values(avg_return_pct) as avg_return_pct values(temp_max_c) as temp_max_c values(rain_mm) as rain_mm by day city
| where mvcount(avg_return_pct)>0 AND mvcount(temp_max_c)>0
| eval avg_return_pct=round(tonumber(mvindex(avg_return_pct,0)),3), temp_max_c=round(tonumber(mvindex(temp_max_c,0)),1), rain_mm=round(tonumber(mvindex(rain_mm,0)),2)
| eval heat_bucket=case(temp_max_c>=30,"Hot", temp_max_c>=20,"Warm", temp_max_c>=10,"Mild", 1=1,"Cold")
| stats count as trading_days avg(avg_return_pct) as mean_return_pct avg(temp_max_c) as mean_temp_c avg(rain_mm) as mean_rain_mm by city heat_bucket
| eval mean_return_pct=round(mean_return_pct,3), mean_temp_c=round(mean_temp_c,1), mean_rain_mm=round(mean_rain_mm,2)
| sort city heat_bucket`}></SplunkSearch>

**How to read the result**
- `trading_days` shows sample size per city and weather bucket.
- `mean_return_pct` compares average day return in each temperature regime.
- `mean_rain_mm` gives a second weather context for that regime.

**Interpretation guidance**
- Start by trusting buckets with larger `trading_days`.
- Compare patterns inside each city first (different markets have different behavior).
- Use this as hypothesis generation, not causality proof.

### Example 2 — Next-day return after rainy vs dry day

Question: **After a rainy day, is the next trading day return different?**

<SplunkSearch earliest="-10y" latest="now" spl={`index=s4c_stock_indices
| eval city=mvindex(exchange_city,0), day=coalesce(mvindex(date,0), strftime(_time, "%Y-%m-%d")), o=tonumber(mvindex(open,0)), c=tonumber(mvindex(close,0)), day_return_pct=((c-o)/o)*100
| stats avg(day_return_pct) as day_return_pct by day city
| append [ search index=s4c_meteo_historic
    | eval day=coalesce(mvindex(date,0), strftime(_time, "%Y-%m-%d")), city=mvindex(city,0), rain_mm=tonumber(precipitation_sum)
    | stats avg(rain_mm) as rain_mm by day city ]
| stats values(day_return_pct) as day_return_pct values(rain_mm) as rain_mm by day city
| where mvcount(day_return_pct)>0 AND mvcount(rain_mm)>0
| eval day_return_pct=mvindex(day_return_pct,0), rain_mm=mvindex(rain_mm,0), rainy_day=if(rain_mm>=5,1,0)
| sort 0 city day
| streamstats current=f window=1 last(rainy_day) as prev_rainy_day by city
| where isnotnull(prev_rainy_day)
| eval scenario=if(prev_rainy_day=1,"After rainy day (>=5mm)","After dry day (<5mm)")
| stats count as observations avg(day_return_pct) as avg_next_day_return_pct stdev(day_return_pct) as stdev_next_day_return by city scenario
| eval avg_next_day_return_pct=round(avg_next_day_return_pct,3), stdev_next_day_return=round(stdev_next_day_return,3)
| sort city scenario`}></SplunkSearch>

**How it works**
- `streamstats ... last(rainy_day)` creates a **lag feature** per city.
- Today’s return is grouped by **yesterday’s rain condition**.

**Interpretation guidance**
- `avg_next_day_return_pct` compares the two scenarios.
- `stdev_next_day_return` shows variability/risk around that average.
- `observations` prevents over-interpreting tiny groups.

### Example 3 — Fast baseline with `tstats`: rain bucket vs market activity

Question: **Does broad market event activity differ by rainfall regime?**

<SplunkSearch earliest="-10y" latest="now" spl={`| tstats count as stock_events where index=s4c_stock_indices by _time span=1d
| eval day=strftime(_time, "%Y-%m-%d")
| stats sum(stock_events) as stock_events by day
| append [ search index=s4c_meteo_historic
    | eval day=coalesce(mvindex(date,0), strftime(_time, "%Y-%m-%d")), rain_mm=tonumber(precipitation_sum)
    | stats avg(rain_mm) as avg_rain_mm by day ]
| stats values(stock_events) as stock_events avg(avg_rain_mm) as avg_rain_mm by day
| where mvcount(stock_events)>0 AND isnotnull(avg_rain_mm)
| eval stock_events=tonumber(mvindex(stock_events,0))
| eval rain_bucket=case(avg_rain_mm>=10,"Heavy rain (>=10mm)", avg_rain_mm>=5,"Rainy (5-9.9mm)", avg_rain_mm>0,"Light rain (0-4.9mm)", 1=1,"No rain")
| stats count as days avg(stock_events) as avg_stock_events by rain_bucket
| eval avg_stock_events=round(avg_stock_events,1)
| sort -avg_stock_events`}></SplunkSearch>

**Why this is useful in class**
- Demonstrates when `tstats` is ideal: fast daily baseline from indexed data.
- Shows a practical mixed approach: `tstats` for activity + event search for weather.

**Interpretation guidance**
- This is a macro-level signal (activity volume), not price direction.
- Use it as a pre-check before deeper return/volatility analysis.

### Teaching notes

- Encourage participants to change thresholds (for example rain `>=3`, `>=8`) and compare stability.
- Ask learners to add `index_name` grouping to see market-specific behavior.
- Extend with month/season grouping (`strftime(day,"%m")`) to test seasonality effects.
