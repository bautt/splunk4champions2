Job Inspector is your microscope for search performance. It tells you what Splunk actually did, how long each part took, and what it had to chew through to give you a result.

Open it from any search via **Job → Inspect Job**, or directly with `/manager/splunk4champions2/job_inspector?sid=<your_sid>`. For scheduled or saved searches that ran without you watching, find them under **Activity → Jobs** and open the inspector from there.

---

## What you see — three sections

### 1. The overview (top of the page)

The headline: total runtime, **scanned event count in bold**, and any warnings or errors. If `scanCount` is dramatically larger than the result count, this search has optimisation potential — start digging.

### 3. Job properties (bottom)

Alphabetical list. The fields you will reach for first:

- `eventCount`, `scanCount`, `resultCount` — how much work for how much output
- `runDuration` — wall-clock time
- `earliestTime`, `latestTime` — the actual time range Splunk searched
- `optimizedSearch` — the rewritten SPL after Splunk's own optimisations
- `sid` — search id; copy this to share or to pop straight into the inspector
- Search log links at the very bottom — search-head log and per-indexer logs for deep dives

## The two diagnostic questions

Whenever a search looks slow, the article by [Brügmann & Müller (Consist)](https://www.consist.de/de/unternehmen/blog/artikel/Splunk-Suchen-verstehen-mit-dem-Job-Inspector/) frames optimisation as two questions:

> **Can I express my filter criteria differently?**
>
> **Can I reach my goal a different way?**

Both questions assume you already know the answer is faster — your job in the Inspector is to find the evidence that proves it.
